# Curriculum Resource, Assessment, and Proctoring Standard

**Status:** Draft. Faculty, assessment, accessibility, privacy, academic-integrity, legal, and Board approval required.

Each approved course owns a curriculum map linking every course learning outcome to instruction, textbook chapters, workbooks, references, software, supplies, assignments, quizzes, discussions, case studies, practical work, midterm, final, optional extra credit, and program outcomes. Costs, licenses, accessibility, prerequisites, instructional minutes, expected student effort, and assessment weights are disclosed before enrollment.

## 221-item bank standard

Each course maintains a target bank of 221 independently reviewed items: 55 single-choice, 35 multiple-select, 30 calculations, 30 applied scenarios, 20 case analyses, 20 short responses, 12 writing tasks, 10 structured discussions, 6 practical/workbook items, and 3 oral defenses. This is a bank, not a requirement that one exam contain 221 questions. Faculty select an appropriate form through an approved blueprint measuring outcomes and cognitive levels without excessive testing burden.

Every item stores outcome, cognitive level, type, difficulty, rationale, authoritative citations, scoring key or rubric, accessibility review, version, author, reviewer, exposure status, and psychometric results. AI-drafted items remain drafts until subject-matter and assessment faculty approve them. Live answer keys are encrypted and never exposed through public APIs.

## Assessment forms

Quizzes, exams, finals, writing assignments, discussions, cases, practicals, extra credit, test-out exams, and standardized forms have published points, weights, timing, attempts, permitted materials, accommodations, integrity rules, grading method, feedback schedule, and appeal rights. Extra credit cannot be required and cannot substitute for demonstrating a required outcome. Test-out credit uses a separately approved secure form and does not imply transferability.

## Proctoring

Proctoring is risk-based, privacy-minimizing, accommodation-aware, and disclosed before enrollment. Identity checks, live review, recording, automated flags, retention, vendor access, incident response, and alternate arrangements require policy and contract approval. An automated flag is not proof of misconduct. A trained human reviews evidence before action, and the student receives notice and appeal rights.
