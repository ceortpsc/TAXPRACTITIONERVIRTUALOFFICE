# Academic Affairs Manual

## Academic authority

The Provost is accountable for academic quality. The Academic Council recommends policy. Curriculum committees review programs and courses using academic need, authority, learning coherence, faculty capacity, library/resources, accessibility, assessment, technology, financial sustainability, student protection, and teach-out criteria. Board approval is required for new degrees, material mission change, and other reserved matters.

## Curriculum committee records

Minutes record meeting number, date, attendance, quorum, recusals, agenda, proposal identifiers, evidence reviewed, motions, votes, conditions, effective term, responsible owner, and follow-up. Student-specific information and privileged advice are excluded from public minutes. Approved minutes are immutable; corrections are appended.

## Course approval form

- Proposal ID, academic unit, proposer and effective term
- Course code, title, level, credits/contact hours and delivery mode
- Catalog description, prerequisites/corequisites and repeatability
- Rationale, program mapping, PLO/CLO alignment and curriculum map
- Weekly instructional-time calculation and student-work estimate
- Required faculty qualifications and support capacity
- Coursebook/OER/library resources and copyright clearance
- Assessments, rubrics, mastery thresholds and integrity controls
- Accessibility review and alternative formats
- Technology, privacy, AI, proctoring and vendor impacts
- Budget, teach-out and implementation plan
- Department, curriculum committee, Provost and regulatory approvals

## Program approval form

Adds market/student need, award level, CIP/SOC mapping, admission and completion rules, term-by-term plan, transfer assumptions, faculty roster, library analysis, facilities/technology, student services, learning outcomes, assessment plan, enrollment and financial projections, refund/cancellation terms, complaint process, authorization/accreditation analysis, licensure disclosure, teach-out, records custody, and external review.

## Syllabus standard

Every syllabus includes institution and course identity; instructor and support contacts; description; credits/contact hours; prerequisites; outcomes; required resources; weekly schedule; assessments and weights; grading scale; attendance/participation; late/recovery rules; integrity and AI-use rules; accessibility and accommodation route; nondiscrimination/Title IX reporting; privacy; technical requirements; communication expectations; complaint/appeal route; and change-control statement.

## Faculty credentialing

Files include identity verification, CV, official transcripts obtained securely from issuing institutions, licenses/certifications where required, subject-matter rationale, teaching evidence, professional experience, references, background determination when lawful, annual disclosure, appointment scope, evaluation, development, and separation date. Equivalent-experience decisions require written rubric, independent review, and Provost approval. AI personas are not faculty of record.

## Credit hour assignment

Credit is based on verified student learning and an approved equivalency methodology—not seat time alone. Each proposal documents instructional interaction, independent work, assessments, expected time, modality, accelerated-term equivalence, and periodic validation using learner analytics and faculty review. No credit is assigned merely for AI conversation or platform activity.

## Distance learning

Online courses require regular and substantive interaction as applicable, authenticated participation, accessible content, qualified faculty oversight, orientation, technical support, identity verification, privacy notice, predictable response standards, academic engagement records, and equivalent outcomes. Any identity-verification charge must be disclosed before enrollment.

## Academic integrity

Prohibited conduct includes unauthorized assistance, fabrication, plagiarism, impersonation, collusion, tampering, and misuse of protected assessment content. Permitted AI use is stated per assessment. Detection tools create signals, not findings. Students receive notice, evidence, response opportunity, impartial human review, proportionate outcome, and appeal. Records are access-limited and retained under schedule.

## Grading and standing

Faculty-approved rubrics govern grades. Objective automation is reviewed statistically and contestable. Incomplete, withdrawal, repeat, grade correction, academic warning, probation, suspension, dismissal, reinstatement, and appeal rules use published thresholds and due process. Satisfactory academic progress for federal aid remains disabled until Title IV authority and an approved SAP policy exist.

## Faculty evaluation

Annual evaluation considers teaching effectiveness, student learning, course stewardship, timely feedback, accessibility, professional development, service, scholarship/practice, integrity, and improvement commitments. Student ratings are one source and never the sole determinant. The faculty member may respond and appeal.

## Academic calendar

The Registrar publishes application, registration, add/drop, census, withdrawal, assessment, grade, conferment, holiday, and appeal dates only after operational and regulatory approval. Changes preserve instructional equivalence and student notice.

## Standard institutional rubric

- Advanced (4): accurate, integrated, source-grounded, ethical, transferable application
- Proficient (3): accurate and complete with supported application
- Developing (2): partial accuracy or weak support requiring revision
- Beginning (1): major gaps, unsupported claims, or unsafe application
- No evidence (0): missing, nonresponsive, or unverifiable

Programs define measurable PLOs in disciplinary knowledge, inquiry, communication, quantitative/digital fluency, ethical judgment, cultural/community responsibility, and applied integration. Courses map CLOs to PLOs and identify introduced, reinforced, and mastered levels.
