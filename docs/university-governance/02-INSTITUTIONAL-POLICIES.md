# Institutional Policies Handbook

## Policy governance

Policies follow proposal, impact review, stakeholder consultation, legal/compliance review, approval, publication, training, monitoring, review, revision, and retirement. Exceptions require documented authority, reason, duration, compensating controls, and approval. No AI agent may approve policy or an exception.

## Risk and internal controls

The institution uses an enterprise risk register covering authority/accreditation, academic quality, enrollment, student protection, financial sustainability, privacy, cybersecurity, accessibility, civil rights, vendors, continuity, records, and reputation. Each risk has inherent rating, controls, owner, residual rating, indicators, treatment, due date, and assurance evidence.

Control design uses segregation of duties, least privilege, dual approval, reconciliations, immutable audit events, vendor due diligence, change management, backups, recovery tests, incident escalation, and quarterly management certification. Material control failures reach the Audit and Risk Committee without management interference.

## Records retention and archiving

The records schedule distinguishes permanent governance and credential records; long-term academic and financial records; time-limited applications, assessments, complaints, accommodations, investigations, contracts, security logs, and operational data; and legal holds. Destruction requires an expired schedule, absence of hold, owner authorization, auditable disposition, and secure deletion. Retention periods are finalized by counsel against applicable federal, Texas, accreditor, contractual, and limitation requirements.

## FERPA and education records

If and when FERPA applies, eligible students receive annual notice and rights to inspect records, request amendment, consent to disclosure, and complain to the U.S. Department of Education. Access is limited to authenticated officials with legitimate educational interest. Disclosures rely on consent or a documented exception, and required disclosure records are maintained. Directory information is not designated until a separate notice, opt-out process, data inventory, and risk review are approved.

The institution will apply equivalent privacy controls contractually even before federal funding status is resolved. FERPA is not a cybersecurity standard; security, minimization, encryption, retention, vendor, and incident controls remain separately required.

## ADA, Section 504 and accessibility

Programs are designed for equivalent access. The institution appoints an accessibility coordinator; publishes a request and grievance process; provides reasonable modifications and auxiliary aids; conducts individualized review; prohibits retaliation; and offers accessible alternatives without lowering essential academic requirements. Digital content targets WCAG 2.2 AA, subject to legal review and applicable requirements. Procurement requires accessibility documentation and testing.

## Title IX and nondiscrimination

The institution prohibits sex discrimination where applicable, appoints a qualified Title IX Coordinator before operation, publishes contact and reporting channels, uses prompt and equitable procedures under the then-current binding regulations, provides supportive measures, addresses conflicts and retaliation, preserves impartial decision-making, and trains relevant personnel. Counsel must verify the controlling rule before adoption; legal templates are not frozen into software.

## Safety, emergency response and continuity

The plan covers online threats, self-harm/welfare escalation, identity compromise, cyber incidents, severe weather affecting operations, workplace emergencies, vendor outage, loss of records, and communication failure. It defines incident command, emergency contacts, severity, notification authority, accessibility, continuity priorities, backup channels, recovery objectives, exercises, after-action review, and corrective action.

Clery applicability depends on institutional and Title IV facts and any covered geography. The institution will not publish invented crime statistics or claim Clery compliance without a documented determination and required evidence.

## Data governance and privacy

Data are classified public, internal, confidential, restricted, and regulated. Owners define purpose, lawful basis, fields, minimum access, retention, sharing, lineage, quality, and disposition. Sensitive student, identity, disability, financial, and disciplinary information is excluded from AI prompts by default. Cross-tenant access is denied server-side.

## IT security for LMS, SIS and identity

Mandatory controls include managed identities, MFA, organization-scoped RBAC, privileged-access review, encryption in transit and at rest, key rotation, secure SDLC, dependency and secret scanning, logging with redaction, vulnerability management, tested backup/recovery, incident response, vendor review, session revocation, device and administrator safeguards, immutable audit correlation, and annual tabletop testing.

AI systems operate server-side with approved sources, prompt limits, redaction, rate limits, human review, model/vendor inventory, evaluation, misuse monitoring, and a kill switch. They cannot confer credentials, decide discipline, approve accommodations, disclose records, or make final admissions/employment decisions.
