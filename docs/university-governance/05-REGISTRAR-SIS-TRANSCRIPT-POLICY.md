# Registrar, SIS and Transcript Policy

## Authority and records

The Registrar is custodian of the official academic record and controls term, course, enrollment, grade, credit, standing, completion, conferment, transcript, verification, and records-correction processes. Faculty submit grades; only authorized Registrar workflows post or correct the official record. Administrators and AI tools cannot silently overwrite it.

## SIS minimum record

Institution and tenant; student identifier; verified legal name and approved chosen/preferred name; contact and identity-verification status; admission basis; program/catalog year; term/enrollment status; course section; modality; credits attempted/earned; grade; repeat/withdrawal/incomplete indicators; transfer source/evaluation; GPA; academic standing; holds; completion audit; award; conferment date; credential ID; transcript events; consent/disclosure log; corrections; and audit correlation.

Sensitive identity documents, disability records, conduct evidence, financial-aid files, and authentication secrets are segregated from the ordinary academic record with narrower access.

## Transcript format

Official transcripts display institutional legal name and authority disclosure; document legend; student name and identifier sufficient for matching without exposing SSN; issue date; attendance dates; program/major; courses by term; credits; grades; GPA; transfer summary; academic standing where policy permits; awards and conferment date; authentication/security feature; Registrar attestation; and transcript key.

The transcript legend defines calendar, credit unit, grading scale, repeat/withdrawal/incomplete marks, GPA rules, transfer credit, academic level, delivery notations if used, accreditation/authorization statement, and authenticity verification. Proposed programs cannot generate an “official” transcript or degree notation before authority and board gates pass.

## Issuance and verification

Requests require authenticated identity, consent where applicable, fee disclosure, hold analysis under applicable law, delivery choice, recipient confirmation, and audit event. Electronic transcripts use an approved secure network or signed document with expiration and tamper evidence. Email attachments are not the default secure method.

## Corrections and appeals

Clerical corrections require source evidence and dual control. Grade changes require faculty/academic authority and reason. Historical changes append an immutable event and preserve prior value. Students receive published review and appeal routes. No record is changed to satisfy an employer, court, lender, or other third party without lawful authority.

## Conferment gate

The graduation audit verifies program/catalog, credits, residency, GPA, required courses, practicum/capstone, academic and conduct status, record accuracy, applicable financial clearance, institutional/program authority, and human approval. Credential numbering occurs only after approval. Revocation requires counsel-reviewed grounds, notice, evidence, hearing/response opportunity, final human decision, and permanent audit record.

## Reporting and clearinghouse

Enrollment, completion, DegreeVerify, DiplomaVerify, NSLDS, state, and accreditor files remain disabled until the institution has applicable authority, contracts, identifiers, specifications, privacy basis, tested secure transport, reporting calendar, certified owners, acknowledgement reconciliation, correction procedures, and incident response. Every batch is hashed, validated, approved, transmitted once, acknowledged, reconciled, and archived.

## Retention and continuity

Award, conferment, transcript, and credential-verification records are designated permanent unless counsel approves a different lawful schedule. The institution maintains encrypted backups, geographic resilience, restoration testing, successor custodian/closure arrangements, and a teach-out records plan.
